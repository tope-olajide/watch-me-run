# Run loop audit

Generated by `node tools/run-audit.mjs 120 1500` — 120 simulated runs of 1500 m in each world,
using the real pattern field, obstacle kinds, coin placement, speed ramp, and difficulty curve.
The raw per-world output is in `doc/run-loop-audit-data.md`.

The harness models a runner that plans a route through each pattern: it groups hazards into rows,
works out how long the player has to reach a safe lane, and checks whether the required move fits
inside that window. It reports what was *asked for*, so a number like "unavoidable" is a claim about
the content, not about the simulator.

## Summary

Fairness is clean, coin placement is fixed, the dead air between decisions has been closed, the three
worlds run at their own pace, and no row asks for more lane changes than its lead-in pays for.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| desert | 8.6 → 17.0 m/s over 320 m | 820 m | 15.7 | 3.5 / 100m | 1.5 / 100m | 3.12s | 0.87s | **0.00 / run** | 39% | 13.2 / 100m | **0** | 0 |
| city | 7.4 → 14.0 m/s over 260 m | 520 m | 13.2 | 3.9 / 100m | 1.6 / 100m | 3.45s | 1.07s | **0.00 / run** | 37% | 14.3 / 100m | **0** | 0 |
| forest | 7.8 → 15.2 m/s over 520 m | 700 m | 13.5 | 3.6 / 100m | 1.5 / 100m | 3.45s | 0.98s | **0.00 / run** | 38% | 13.6 / 100m | **0** | 0 |

The shares move by a point or two and the medians by a tenth of a second between runs, since each run
generates its own content; the counts that matter here are the zeros.

- **Nothing is unavoidable.** Zero trapped rows, zero windows under 0.35 s, zero jump/slide pairs
  inside the previous clip, zero coins behind a wall, zero coins that cost you a hit — in all three
  worlds, at all three top speeds.
- **The worlds are different runs, not one run repainted.** Speed and spacing trade against each
  other on purpose (see below), so the densest world is the slowest and the fastest is the emptiest.
- **No row closes two lanes at once.** The rows that read two lanes are gone — **0.00 per run** in all
  three worlds, down from 0.10–0.15 — because a `pair` is built with at most one wall now (see "Fixed"
  below). The `patternGap` floor that gives such a row the lead-in it would need is still in place and
  now never binds, so the invariant is measured rather than assumed.
- **Decisions arrive closer together.** Actionable rows went 1.4–1.5 → 1.5–1.7 per 100 m and the gap
  between them fell from 3.3–3.5 s to 3.1–3.5 s, with the opening band improved most (4.6–5.0 s →
  3.8–4.0 s). The shape-spacing work below then spends ~0.1–0.2 s of that back in the two slower
  worlds to buy much wider windows — a deliberate trade, and the summary table above is the state
  after it.
- **No row arrives closer than a lane change.** `slalom`, `gauntlet`, and `tunnel` were widened at
  the source (see below), so the tightest row in any world is **0.87 s** against 0.63 s before.
- **The back half pays better.** Coin *density* is flat by design, so the value of a token now rises
  with difficulty: **25 points at the line → 75 once a world's content peaks**, taking coin points per
  100 m from ~380 in the opening band to ~1040–1160 in the last full one.

## Fixed: coins now reward the required move

Coins used to be placed by shape at a lane chosen with `otherLane()` — a coin flip — so a coin often
sat exactly where the next wall would arrive:

```
before:  desert 213, city 204, forest 240 coins per run were a wall trap,
         e.g. coin-then-wall at 1348m: coin y=1.05 lane 2, wall inside the escape window [slalom]
after:   0 in all three worlds, and 0 coins behind a wall
```

`src/game/pattern-field.ts` now places coins **after** the obstacles of a shape, in a lane the
required move leaves the runner in, and only if no wall will cross that lane within an escape window
of any coin in the line (`coinLanes` / `pickCoinLane`). The reward is shaped by the move too:

| The obstacle asks for | The reward is | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a jump (`block`) | an arc over it, in its lane | collected mid-jump, at the apex |
| a slide (`gate`) | a low line under the bar | collected while sliding |
| a lane change (`wall`) | a line in the destination lane | collected after the dodge |
| a slalom | a line in the lane open across *two* turns | pays for reading it, not for leaving late |

Only walls disqualify a lane. A block or a gate is passable, so coins beside one are the reward for
the jump or slide it already asked for — which is why the mid-air share rose from 17% to 19–22%.
A pattern whose every lane is spoken for simply carries no coins.

Two things were changed in the harness so it measures placement rather than spacing:

- The on-route share used to match each coin to the nearest *row* within ±0.9 s, so a coin between
  two rows could never count. It now carries the route's lane forward from the last row it passed.
- Note what that share means: the route is *one* optimal path, not the only one. A coin off it is an
  opt-in pickup, not an unreachable one — and the wall checks are what make it fair. (An
  "out of reach" metric was written and then deleted: it measured the distance from the planner's
  lane, which is not the only lane a player may hold, so it was noise.)

Verified in the game as well as the model: a headless 40 s run through the new field still collects
tokens (`2 → 4 → 6 → 10 → 12`), with no console errors and no collisions.

## Fixed: the dead air between decisions

Two mechanisms, because there were two causes.

**Gaps now close after a chunk that asked for nothing.** `patternGap()` takes the pattern just 
placed and uses the shorter gap when it demanded no move. "Demanded" is an intrinsic property of the
content, decided when it is built (`isDemanding`): a wall cannot be passed in place, and a row that
blocks two lanes leaves only one. Asking whether the *player* was threatened is not available — the
generator does not know their lane.

The gap cannot close blindly: a pattern's coins must not end up inside the escape window of the next
pattern's first hazard, so each pattern now reports its own `tail` (the empty distance after its last
piece) and the gap is floored at `escape − tail`. The soft gap is also floored at one lane change at
top speed, which is how a pacing change could otherwise become an unfair row.

**The late-game mix asks for more.** A lone obstacle in one of three lanes asks for nothing two times
out of three — the runner is simply not in that lane. After difficulty 0.5 the shape mix now leans on
chunks that block two lanes or carry a wall (`SHAPES_LATE`), which took the share of chunks asking
for nothing from 57% to 46%.

| | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| actionable rows per run | 21.2 / 22.6 / 22.4 | 25.0 / 25.2 / 25.5 |
| dead air median | 3.52 / 3.27 / 3.27s | 3.07 / 3.00 / 3.00s |
| decision cadence median | 1.82 / 1.70 / 1.77s | 1.75 / 1.68 / 1.75s |
| chunks asking nothing | 57 / 58 / 58% | 48 / 47 / 46% |
| band windows, 0m → 1400m | 4.97 → 1.55s | 3.98 → 1.41s |
| violations / trapped / collisions | 0 / 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 / 0 |

An earlier pass weighted `gauntlet` twice in the late mix; it reached 3.07 s as well but made 40% of
late rows gauntlets, so it was rebalanced toward pairs and arcs. Same dead air, more variety.

Verified in the game, not only the model: a headless 40 s run over the new field reaches 528 m,
collects 15 tokens, takes one stumble, and logs no errors or warnings.

## Each world now runs at its own pace

The dead-air work left all three worlds on one ramp — mean speed 14.5 m/s each, differing only in
obstacle kinds. They now have their own pace and spacing (`environmentPace` and `gapScale` in
`src/game/pattern-field.ts`), and the two trade against each other deliberately:

| | desert | city | forest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Speed ramp | 8.6 → 17.0 m/s over 320 m | 7.4 → 14.0 m/s over 260 m | 7.8 → 15.2 m/s over 520 m |
| Content peaks at | 820 m | 520 m | 700 m |
| Gap scale | 1.08 | 0.78 | 0.96 |
| Obstacle kinds | block-heavy | gate-heavy | balanced |
| Reads as | open road, quick and sparse | crowded from the start, but slower | a long climb that keeps tightening |

Because speed and spacing move in opposite directions, the worlds land in similar *time* windows from
different places — the city is densest per 100 m (3.9 decision rows, 14.5 coins) and still has the
longest minimum window (1.07 s), while the desert has the fewest rows (3.6) and the tightest window
(0.87 s) because it arrives fastest. The forest spends its difference on the ramp instead: slowest to
build, so the second half keeps getting harder.

It also moves where "late game" begins: the shape mix turns sharp at difficulty 0.5, which is 410 m
in the desert, 260 m in the city, and 350 m in the forest.

Verified in the game, per world, with the harness's own world selector:

```
WORLD=city  node tools/orbis-probe.mjs  → HUD SPEED caps at 14.0, runSpeed 1.000, 29 tokens in 40 s
            node tools/orbis-probe.mjs  → HUD SPEED caps at 17.0, runSpeed 1.000, 22 tokens in 40 s
both runs: Live world streaming throughout, 0 errors, 0 warnings
```

The speed constants used to be copied into the harness, which would have silently measured the
wrong ramp once they became per-world; the harness now imports `speedAt` / `difficultyAt` / `topSpeed`
from the field the scene uses, the same way it already imported the difficulty curve.

## Fixed: no row arrives closer than a lane change

`slalom` was the tightest read in the game: three turns 11 m apart, which is **0.65 s** at the
desert's top speed and 0.68 s as measured. The harness's own model spends ~0.5 s on a lane change, so
there was almost no margin for a player who reacts late — and `slalom` plus `gauntlet` were 49% of all
rows in that band.

All three were fixed at the source, by distance rather than by tuning numbers:

| Shape | Turn spacing | Rows / run (desert) | Share of rows | Tightest window |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| slalom | 11 m → **16 m** | 14.6 → 5.8 | 24% → **11%** | 0.68 → **0.93 s** |
| gauntlet | 12 m → **15 m** | 15.6 → 16.8 | 25% → 31% | 0.75 → **0.87 s** |
| tunnel (gate → obstacle) | 13 m → **16 m** | 12.5 → 7.2 | 20% → 13% | 0.80 → **0.93 s** |

The gauntlet and the tunnel had the same problem one step behind the slalom, so widening only the
slalom would have moved the tightest read rather than removing it. With all three widened, **no row in
any world now arrives closer than 0.87 s** (0.87 / 1.07 / 0.98), against 0.63 / 0.78 / 0.72 before.

Cutting `slalom` to 11% of rows removed decisions along with it, so the late mix leans harder on
`pair` — one row, two lanes blocked, which is a decision without being a twitch. The result is that
*fewer* chunks ask for nothing than before this change (38% vs 46%):

| | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| tightest row (desert / city / forest) | 0.63 / 0.78 / 0.72 s | **0.87 / 1.07 / 0.98 s** |
| slalom share of rows | 24% | 11% |
| chunks asking nothing | 46 / 45 / 45% | 38 / 35 / 36% |
| dead air median | 2.95 / 3.30 / 3.32 s | 3.12 / 3.30 / 3.47 s |
| violations / trapped / collisions / coin traps | 0 | 0 |

The one cost is honest and small: wider shapes are longer chunks, so decisions are ~0.1–0.2 s further
apart in the two slower worlds. That is the trade taken deliberately — a 0.63 s read is a lost run for
a human, a 3.1 s gap between decisions is not.

Verified in the game in all three worlds, not only in the model, with the probe's own world selector
(`WORLD=city node tools/orbis-probe.mjs …`, which clicks that world's card before the run starts):

### Checked at the phone breakpoint too

The run is not desktop-only — the HUD, the world chip and the director card are absolutely positioned,
which is exactly the layout that breaks on a phone. So the probe can hold the phone viewport for the
whole session (`MOBILE=1 node tools/orbis-probe.mjs …`, 430×900) and report, at the end of a run, what

the HUD's `3`, *the status chip was covering the player's numbers*. The two are now stacked at this
breakpoint — chip directly under the header (`top: 72`), HUD below it (`top: 104`) — and the overlap

One thing the phone check cannot tell you: the *frames* at 430×900 are cropped from the same 2560×1440
generated video, so what changes at this breakpoint is the interface around the world, not the world's
resolution. That is the composition the desktop pass already measures.

## Fixed: the reward curve rises with difficulty

Coin **density** is deliberately flat — 11–15 tokens per 100 m in every band, from the line to 1400 m
— because density is a *fairness* property: it is what `tools/run-audit.mjs` measures, and thickening
the late bands would have meant re-checking every coin-placement rule. What was missing was the other
half: with flat density and a rising speed, the reward per metre never went up, so there was no reason
to reach 800 m and no reason to take the risky line when you got there. The content asked for more and
paid the same.

So the *value* of a token now scales with difficulty, in the field module rather than the scene, so the
same number is measurable:

```ts
coinValueAt(environment, distance)   // 25 pts at the line → 75 pts once content peaks
```

| | desert | city | forest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Token value, at the line → at the peak | 25 → 75 | 25 → 75 | 25 → 75 |
| Coin points / 100 m, opening band | 380 | 385 | 386 |
| Coin points / 100 m, last full band | **1038** | **1163** | **1099** |

Every band, as points per run per 200 m (desert, latest pass): 831 · 1050 · 1348 · 1788 · 2048 · 2059 ·
2046 — a curve that climbs with the run instead of a flat line. The band-to-band numbers move by a few percent
between runs, like every other share here. The HUD carries it too: the token count now
reads `TOKENS 27 ×2.4`, so the ramp is visible to the player rather than only in the scoring code.

Confirmed in the game, not just the model, in **all three worlds**. An 80 s desert run first, read off the
HUD's own `×` value (the live `coinMultiplierAt`):

**The value curve itself, derived from the live HUD rather than read off the code.** Score is 12 points
per *second* plus token value, so `score − 12 × elapsed` is the points that came from tokens; differencing
that against the token count gives what each token actually paid, on the machine, mid-run:

| | first tokens | | at the cap | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| city | 45.0 pts/token @ 223 m | ×1.9 | 75.0 @ 568 m | ×3.0 |

collects whatever the runner's route happened to touch — the city pass gained 0 tokens between 775 m and

`330x39` with `headerOverChip: 0` and the HUD to `330x137@104,15`, and at 430 nothing moved.

One defect found while verifying, **since fixed**: when `connect()` itself fails on a transport error the
message reached the player verbatim (`http transport error: jwt resolver rejected: fetch failed`),
because the connect-failure path set the error directly instead of passing it through the
`TRANSPORT_PATTERN` filter that `WorldLayer` documents for every other failure.

`failureMessage()` now classifies both paths, and the honest copy depends on *where* the failure
happened: a link that was once live is being retried by the recovery effect and reads "World link
interrupted — reconnecting", while a link that never came up has nothing retrying it, so calling that a
reconnect would be a lie and it reads "Couldn't reach the Orbis world — the run plays in local world
mode". The second branch turns out to be triggerable by breaking the session's token route on purpose,
which is how it was checked rather than assumed: the chip showed the friendly copy on all four samples,
and the raw SDK string appeared only in the console's `[orbis] connect failed` line, where a diagnosis
belongs.

Fairness is untouched, which was the point of changing value instead of density: violations, trapped
rows, move collisions and coin traps are all still **0** in all three worlds, and the coin-per-run
economy is unchanged (13.3 / 14.5 / 13.8 per 100 m).

`tools/run-audit.mjs` now reports the curve, because a reward curve nobody measures is a claim: it
prints the value at the line and at the peak, coin points per 100 m for the opening and last *full*
band (the trailing band is cut by the end of the run and would read as a collapse), and points per
band in the data file.

## Fixed: the rows that read two lanes

Only a `pair` can ask for two lane changes at once, and two facts pin that down: a lane is only ever
closed by a `wall`, and a row holding a single obstacle therefore can never take the runner's own lane
*and* the one beside it — so closing two lanes at once needs two obstacles in one row, which is `pair`
and nothing else. The harness now attributes every two-lane read to the shape behind it, and across 120
runs per world it reports `[pair …]` and nothing else, which is what makes the rule checkable rather
than assumed.

Those rows were held to the field's *single*-change floor, so a player standing at the far end of one
was asked to read it and cross two lanes inside the time the content gives for one. `patternGap` now
takes the shape of the chunk that is *coming* and, when that chunk can close two lanes, holds the gap at
`REACTION_S + 2 × LANE_CHANGE_S` at the fastest speed any world reaches (22.95 m). Everything else keeps
the pacing gap, so the cost falls where the problem is rather than on the whole field.

| | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| lead-in floor for a two-lane row | the single-change window (`ESCAPE_DISTANCE`) | **the two-change budget** |
| which shape the floor was applied to | the previous frame's pick, usually discarded | the shape actually built |
| tightest two-lane window | not measured | **1.35 / 1.63 / 1.98 s** (22.9 / 22.9 / 30.1 m) |
| two-lane rows under the content's 1.35 s | — | **0** |
| dead air median | 3.12 / 3.30 / 3.47 s | 3.12 / 3.50 / 3.42 s |
| violations / trapped / collisions / coin traps | 0 | 0 |

The first cut of this fixed nothing, and the measurement is what said so: the shape picked to size a gap
was re-picked at the top of every frame, so the shape the floor was computed for was usually *not* the
one that got built, and the lead-in belonged to a chunk that never existed. It is held in a ref now — in
the scene and in the harness mirror, since the harness reproduces the spawn loop rather than calling it.

Two further things fell out of the work.

The **cost** is the city's +0.2 s of dead air, and it is the honest price of the densest world holding
its pairs open long enough: the desert and the forest are unchanged within noise. The count of two-lane
rows did not fall — the lever is the window, not the shape — but their minimum lead-in is now the two
lane changes they ask for, which is what a slower player needed.

The **instrument was reading totals as if they were per run.** The `lane changes … (N need two)` line and
the headline's two-lane column summed across every simulated run, so a 60-run sample printed "city 10"
and was written up here as ten rows *per run* — off by the number of runs. Both are per run now, which is
why the honest figure is 0.10–0.15: the rows exist, they are just rare, at roughly one every seven to ten
runs.

### Then the rows were removed, not just paid for

The lead-in above is the *price* of a two-lane read. It turned out the read did not have to exist. A lane
is only closed by a `wall`, so a row holding one obstacle can never close the runner's own lane *and* the
one beside it — closing two at once needs two obstacles in one row, which is `pair` and nothing else, as
the harness's own attribution had just confirmed. A `pair` is built with **at most one wall** now. One
wall keeps the row unavoidable (the runner is still standing in one of the two blocked lanes two times in
three); two would leave a single lane and ask a runner at the far end of it for two lane changes at once.

The alternative — bias the open lane toward the middle — was rejected on the harness's own evidence: a
pair that opens the middle asks a runner already there for nothing at all, and the route holds the middle
by default, so the decisions this shape exists for would have drained away while the dead air came back.
Capping the walls instead costs nothing measurable, because the row still asks the same question:

| | with the lead-in floor | after capping the pair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| two-lane rows per run | 0.10 / 0.15 / 0.13 | **0.00 / 0.00 / 0.00** |
| dead air median | 3.12 / 3.50 / 3.42 s | 3.12 / 3.45 / 3.45 s |
| actionable rows / 100 m | 1.5 / 1.6 / 1.6 | 1.5 / 1.6 / 1.5 |
| violations / trapped / collisions / coin traps | 0 | 0 |

The floor stays. It is the invariant a future two-obstacle shape has to satisfy, and the harness reports
the two-lane rows it finds — so a regression shows up as a number rather than as a run somebody lost.

## Tried and reverted: steering a `pair` around the runner's lane

Since a `pair` blocks two lanes, the open one *is* the decision, so steering it looked free: instead of
letting chance pick the open lane, put it one step from where the previous chunk left the runner — the
row then always asks for a move, and always the cheapest one.

It made the run worse. Measured over the same 120 runs × 1500 m:

| | steered | not steered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| dead air median | 3.32 / 3.58 / 3.70 s | **3.12 / 3.48 / 3.43 s** |
| actionable rows per run | 21.7 / 24.0 / 22.6 | **21.8 / 24.7 / 23.7** |
| two-lane rows | 0.00 | 0.00 |

The reason is in the premise. The steering needs the content to know where the runner is, and it does
not — the run stays in its lane unless a wall forces it out, so a chunk's claim about the lane it left
the runner in is a guess from its own geometry. The harness now measures that guess directly (every
chunk is tagged, and its last row is held against where the route actually is): **37–41%, against 33%
for naming a lane at random.** The claim is barely informed, so steering around it is barely better
than randomness — and worse in one specific way: concentrating the open lane at a fixed offset from the
last claim herds the route into the lanes the following content does not touch, which is the dead air
above.

The measured claim was kept and the steering was not: `Pattern.heldLane` is now reported and checked
rather than consumed, so the next attempt at this starts from how little the content knows. The audit
prints it as `chunk hint`. Anyone re-trying the steering has one line to restore — but the number to
beat is 37–41%, not 33%.

## What still to change, in priority order

### 1. Done — the rows that read two lanes

The denser late mix raised these: rows where the only safe lane is two lanes away. They are legal — the
harness counts zero violations, so every one of them has a window of at least
`reaction + 2 lane changes` — but they were the tightest reads in the game, and the rows a slower player
loses to.

The temptation is to forbid them, which means forcing every pair to leave the middle lane open. That
wouldbackfire: the harness's route holds the middle by default, so a middle-open pair asks for nothing at
all, and the dead-air win above would be undone. The lever is the window, not the shape — and the gap
before a two-lane row is now held at the two-lane budget.

Two changes later, the rows no longer exist at all: a `pair` carries at most one wall, so no row can
close two lanes at once (0.00 per run in every world, no dead-air cost). See "Fixed: the rows that read
two lanes" above — the window floor is still in place as the guard, and it no longer binds.

### 2. Watch that the reward curve does not make late stumbles cheap

Now that a token at 1400 m is worth three times one at the line, a hit late costs more in expectation
than a hit early — which is the intent. What is *not* yet modelled is whether the combination of a
richer back half and a denser late mix pushes a player toward taking a line they cannot read. The
harness measures content, not decisions; the honest check is play, and the report should be revisited
if the late two-lane rows above start showing up as hits in a real session.

## What is already healthy

- Movement mix per world (desert): jump 672, slide 434, dodge 306, free 2105 — all three actions are
  exercised, and the worlds differ (city skews slide at 758 slide vs 454 jump; desert and forest skew
  jump at 672/683 vs 434/449).
- Every shape's spacing is now above the clip length of the animations it asks for, with the gauntlet
  (the jump → slide → dodge sequence) the tightest at 0.87 s and still twice the 0.5 s a lane change
  costs in the harness's model.
- The per-world pace was tuned against these checks rather than by feel: every world's min window,
trapped rows, move collisions and coin traps are re-measured after each change.
